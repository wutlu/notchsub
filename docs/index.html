How it works

Three steps, about a minute to set up.

Click the notch

A panel drops down from the top of your screen and starts listening to system audio.

Play anything

A YouTube video, a Zoom call, a lecture or a podcast. If your Mac plays it, NotchSub can subtitle it.

Read along

The translation appears in large text with the original line below it. Click the notch again to close the panel.

Install NotchSub

Pick whichever you prefer. Both install the same app.

One-line install Recommended

Open Terminal and paste this command. It downloads the latest version into Applications and opens it.

curl -fsSL https://wutlu.github.io/notchsub/install.sh | bash

Download the .dmg

  1. Open NotchSub.dmg and drag the app into Applications.
  2. On first launch, macOS may say it can't verify the developer. Open System Settings → Privacy & Security, then click “Open Anyway”.
  3. Allow “System Audio Recording” when NotchSub asks.

Download .dmg

On first launch NotchSub downloads the Whisper speech model (~630 MB) and optimizes it for your Mac's Neural Engine, which can take several minutes. Later launches take a few seconds.

FAQ

Does it use my microphone?

No. NotchSub uses Core Audio's system audio tap and only hears what your Mac plays. Your own voice is never captured.

Is anything sent to the internet?

Only two one-time downloads: the speech model from Hugging Face, and Apple's language packs. Transcription and translation happen offline after that.

Which Macs are supported?

Any Apple Silicon Mac (M1 or newer) running macOS 15 Sequoia or later. Notch-less Macs and external displays get a small virtual notch at the top of the screen.

Why does macOS warn about an unidentified developer?

NotchSub is a free community project and isn't notarized with a paid Apple Developer ID yet. The one-line installer avoids the warning, or you can approve the app once in System Settings → Privacy & Security.

Which languages can it translate into?

Speech can be recognized in 99 languages. Translation uses Apple's on-device engine, which covers about 20 major languages. The app's own interface follows your Mac's system language.
